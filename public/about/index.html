01What you are looking at

This is the design team's front door for the XCEL learner platform — the next-generation LMS for insurance, mortgage and banking pre-licensing and continuing education. It is a working prototype, not the shipped product: real screens, real interaction, demo data.

Who is who. Each surface of the product has a designer who owns it. The design lead (Jill Moreno) owns the project itself — the code repository on GitHub and its released copy, main — and is the one person who releases work into the product. When this page says "the team", it means the five designers; when it says "the design lead", it means her.

The page has a left-hand rail of sections, in four groups, and a main area showing whichever one is selected. It opens on Prototypes, because that is the thing most people come to see. There is one Hub, behind one password, and everyone — the design team, developers, and you — sees the same thing.

The one distinction that matters

Prototypes is what the team has landed on. Refinement is what the team is still discussing. Everything else on this page is supporting material. If you only remember one thing, remember that.

Prototypes itself has two views, chosen by the Main · Stage switch above its rows. Main is the released product. Stage is what the surface owners have decided and are waiting to release — a preview of the next Main. Same rows either way; the switch picks which build they open.

03Opening and driving the prototype

In Prototypes, leave the switch on Main and click the first row, End-to-End User Experience. The learner dashboard opens with a dark review bar across the top. That bar is scaffolding for reviewers; a learner would never see it. The rows under Work in progress open the same build at a different door — the invite and purchase flow, the learning Home, Compass — and Stage opens the same doors on the build that is waiting to be released, in a new tab.

The Demo Controls

The bar carries a few dropdowns that change who you are looking at, so one prototype can show many situations:

  • Persona — named example learners with different circumstances.
  • Progress — how far through the course the learner is (on track, at risk, and so on). The label is approximate; the page itself shows the exact figure.
  • Education — pre-licensing versus continuing education, which changes the whole shape of the course.
  • Readiness — how prepared for the state exam the learner is. Deliberately separate from Progress: a learner can be far through the course and not ready.

Change them freely. Nothing you flip is saved — the prototype opens in "demo mode", which shows the team's agreed configuration and forgets your changes when you leave. Reload and you are back at the baseline.

The left rail inside the product

Home · Study Plan · Readiness · My Courses · Certificates · Resources · Rubi Insights · Get Help. Each is a real section you can open. Some sections the team has built but chosen not to show on the rail (Learning Path, Browse Catalog, Podcasts among them); they are hidden, not gone, and a designer can show you one on request.

What the data is

The learner, their course and their progress are demo fixtures. Where a number is a real published fact — the New York state's 40 credit hours, the 70% pass mark, PSI's exam fee — it is real and sourced. Where the team has had to guess a figure XCEL has not published, the code marks it as invented and the screen says so rather than presenting it as fact. If a number looks wrong to you, say so: that is exactly the kind of feedback the fixtures exist to draw out.

Worth knowing

Dates in the prototype are anchored to a fixed "today" (11 May 2026) so that nothing ages into "eight months ago" between reviews. A countdown that seems not to move between visits is doing that on purpose.

04Reading Refinement

Each row is a link plus a short note. The note is the designer telling you where to look and what question they are asking. Open the link, find the thing the note names, form a view.

Three things to hold in mind while you look:

  • It is one idea, isolated. A Refinement build is the agreed baseline plus one change, so what you see differing from Prototypes is the thing under discussion.
  • It may be mid-change. The designer might push an update while you are looking. If a page reloads differently, that is why.
  • You see everything the team sees. Since 15 October there is one list, not a public one and a private one. A row the designer has finished discussing is archived — hidden, not deleted — so what is on the board is what is open right now. The pills above the board filter it by designer.

If Refinement is empty, it says "Nothing is being shown for review right now." That is a true statement about this moment, not an error.

05Giving feedback

The Hub has no comment box, on purpose — feedback belongs in the conversation the team is already having, where it can be answered. Send it to whoever shared the link with you, or to the design lead directly.

What makes feedback easy to act on:

  • Say which section and which row. "Refinement → the study streak card" beats "the new thing".
  • Say which settings you had. If it matters, name the Persona, Progress, Education and Readiness you were on. The same screen can look quite different across them.
  • Separate "this is wrong" from "I'd prefer". Both are useful; they are answered differently. A wrong number gets fixed; a preference gets discussed.
  • Screenshots help, with the browser's address bar visible — the address says exactly which build and which page you were on.

06What else is here

Since 15 October there is one Hub for everyone, so the team's working surfaces are on this page too: developer hand-off notes and what has shipped (under Dev Handoff), QA findings, an archive of what was removed and how to bring it back, and the To Do Board — who is on what this week, as columns a card moves across. Nothing is locked; they are just not written for you. One column on the To Do Board is worth knowing: On Stage lists the work that is on Stage and waiting on a release, with the date it got there.

08Questions we get asked

Is this the real product?

No. It is a working prototype built on the same design system the product will use, so what you see is faithful to how it will look and behave — but the data is demo data and nothing you do here affects any learner.

Can I share the link?

Yes, with the password — there is one for the whole Hub, and the design lead gives it out. Please share both with people who are part of the review, not more widely; it is work in progress.

Can I change anything on the Hub page itself?

You can, in a few places — the boards (Refinement, Resources, Research, PRDs, QA Notes, the To Do Board) are authored on the page and nothing stops you. Please don't, unless the team has asked you to: the Hub has no sign-in, so an edit is anonymous and the team cannot tell who to ask about it. Your influence is your feedback, sent to the person who shared the link.

Something I saw last week has gone. Was it rejected?

Not necessarily. A Refinement row goes away when its idea was decided and shared to Stage (look at Prototypes → Stage, or Main once it is released), when it was parked, or when it was archived after discussion. Ask, and the team will tell you which.

What is the difference between Main and Stage?

Main is released; Stage is decided. A designer who owns a surface — Compass, say — puts their finished work on Stage when they are sure of it, and the design lead releases Stage to Main, everything at once. If you are reviewing what the product is, use Main. If you are previewing what it is about to be, use Stage. After a release the two are the same until the next piece lands on Stage.

Why does the prototype open with "?demo=1" in the address?

That switch tells the prototype to show the team's agreed configuration and to forget any settings you change. Keep it in the address when you share a link, so the next person sees the same thing you did.
