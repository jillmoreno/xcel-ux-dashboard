Pace presets — three dates the window already contains

The Goal Tracker offers Aggressive / Recommended / Relaxed as hours per week, and the pace-and-readiness page's first recommendation refused to offer Relaxed at all, because inside a 30-day window the slowest pace that finishes is the recommendation. This page takes the other reading: aim at the whole window, and all three presets become dates the learner already owns. Relaxed is the day before access ends. Recommended is that, minus a buffer. Focused is the storefront's own promise — "prepares you to pass in less than 2 weeks". Nobody chooses a number; they choose which of three dates to aim at, and the pace is derived.

Three demo axes. Course is the whole point — the same three dates cost very different evenings on a 12-lesson course and a 42-lesson one, and section 03 shows that "Relaxed" only earns its name on the short ones. How they study is the reference's Thorough / Average / Quick, kept as a multiplier on minutes-per-lesson. Day of access re-derives everything from a later start. The exam date and the study-plan calendar live in the sheet, not the demo bar, because they are the learner’s inputs rather than ours.

01

The derivation, in one table

Every widget below reads this. A preset is a finish date; lessons remaining over the days to that date gives lessons a week; times minutes-per-lesson (× the study-style factor) gives minutes a week; the fewest nights of 3 / 4 / 5 / 6 that keep the Recommended evening under the strain line gives the nights count — shared by all three presets, so their evenings compare like with like. 02

The card, and what Adjust opens

The card itself operates nothing. It states a pace and offers two buttons; every control that used to sit on it — the three dates, the days a week — has moved into a sheet behind Adjust, along with two things the card was never the place for: the exam date, and building a study plan on the calendar. Open it and change anything; the card behind re-renders.

Two ceilings, and the sheet says which one binds. The access window and the exam date are different limits, and whichever comes first governs the pace — coursework has to be done EXAM_BUFFER_INVENTED days before a sit date, so an exam booked inside the window pulls every preset earlier. Enter one in the sheet and watch the note under the presets change from your access is still the one doing the work to your exam date is the one doing the work. A pace that silently switched ceilings without saying so is how a learner ends up not trusting the number.
The calendar is the only thing that asks which days. Days-a-week is all the pace needs, so that is all the sheet asks for — until the learner switches on Create a study plan, which cannot be built without real weekdays and a time. Ticking days then becomes authoritative and re-prices the evening, and the sheet says so rather than letting the two quietly disagree.

Labels that lie on long courses

"Relaxed" at 42 lessons is still most of an hour a night. Section 04 proposes the label be conditional — keep the word only when the evening is under EASY_MINS_INVENTED, otherwise call the preset what it is: Full window. Whether a learner is better served by a warm label or an accurate one is a question for research, not for this page.

Minutes per lesson vs credit hours

Every evening here prices a lesson at MINS_PER_LESSON_INVENTED. The storefront's 40 credit hours over 42 lessons is ~57 min each. If seat-time is the real figure, every cell in section 04 is ~1.6× too light and the 42-lesson row has no genuinely relaxed option at all.

"Less than 2 weeks" is a marketing claim

Focused aims at it because it is the product's own sentence, but on a 42-lesson course it lands over the strain line. Either the claim assumes a shorter course, or it assumes an evening this page would flag. Worth knowing which.

What happens to the calendar when the pace changes

Sessions are written from the pace at the moment of saving. If the learner later falls behind, or moves their exam, does the product rewrite the sessions already on their Study Plan, add to them, or leave them alone and let the pace line disagree with the calendar? Nothing here answers that, and it is the first thing a second session would hit.

Whose exam date is it

The sheet takes an exam date on trust — typed, unverified, and not booked through us. The walk-through's booking flow would know the real one. If both exist, the product needs a rule for which wins, and the admin roster's Risk column would then be measuring against a date the learner may have invented.

A pace with no ceiling

Continuing-education courses may have no 30-day window at all. Then Relaxed has nothing to aim at, Recommended has nothing to buffer against, and only the exam date — if there is one — gives the presets meaning. The three-preset model may simply not apply there.
