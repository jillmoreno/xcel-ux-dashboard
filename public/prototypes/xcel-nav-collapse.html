Collapsing the rail — the label is the easy part to remove and the hard part to replace

The shipped rail is 252px of a 1440px screen, permanently, on a product whose main job is reading course content. Collapsing it to icons buys back about 13% of the width. What it costs is every word in the navigation, and a tooltip only gives those words back to people who hover.

So this page takes the three usual collapsed treatments and drives the same rail through all of them — the real seven sections, the real tokens, the real "Behind" badge. Use the toggle at the foot of the rail, then hover and tab through it. The keyboard pass is the one that separates them.

01

The rail, collapsing — drive it

The real seven sections, the real tokens, the real badge. Hover an icon for the tooltip; then tab into the rail, which is where these come apart — the readout under the stage is read off the rail itself, so it reports the order you actually get rather than the one the prose claims. Where the collapse control belongs is argued in §03; the axis is live so both can be judged here.

What the toggle is, in markup. One <button> carrying aria-expanded and aria-controls pointed at the nav — not a role="switch", and not two buttons that swap. Its own accessible name says what pressing it will do ("Collapse navigation" / "Expand navigation"), so a screen-reader user is told the action, and aria-expanded separately reports the state.

The thing that survives collapse is the accessible name. Every label in the rail stays in the DOM in every state — collapsed, it moves to a clip-path box instead of being deleted. So each button is named "Study Calendar" whether or not a tooltip ever fires, and the tooltip is a hint rather than the name of the thing. Wiring the name through title or through the tooltip element is the defect this page exists to rule out: it strands anyone not using a pointer.

02

Three treatments, and what each actually costs

They are not three visual styles — they are three different answers to "where do the words live now". A costs them to a hover, B keeps a shortened copy on screen, C moves them into a temporary panel. Pick the card above to drive the stage.

B needs authored short labels, not truncation. "Records & Certificates" does not become "Records &…" — it becomes Records, which is a different string somebody has to write and approve. Every micro-label in the stage is a fourth field on the nav row for exactly that reason. A rail that ellipsises instead is the tell that this step was skipped.

And the short label is not free to be any word. It has to be contained in the full one, because a voice-control user says what they can see: "click For you" has to reach a button whose name contains "For you".WCAG 2.5.3 That constraint bit here — the Explore section was called Recommended with a micro-label of For you, which is a button nobody can ask for out loud. The full label became Recommended for you rather than the micro-label becoming something worse. A smoke assertion now checks every pair, so the next short label that is not a substring of its own full label fails a test instead of shipping.

C's cost is not in the panel, it is in the pointer. A flyout that opens on hover opens on the way past it — moving the cursor from the content to the browser's back button crosses the rail and the panel appears over what you were reading. Everything that follows (an open delay, a close grace period, an exit corridor) is compensation for a trigger nobody asked for. Opening it on click costs one interaction and removes all three.

03

Where the collapse control sits — bottom, but it is close

Both are well-precedented: top is Slack and Notion, bottom is Linear, Figma and Jira. Neither is wrong, and three of the usual arguments turn out not to decide it here. The fourth does. Flip the axis above and read the tab order under the stage.

Tab order
→ bottom
At the top it is genuinely first, because visual order has to match DOM order — so every keyboard pass through the nav starts on a chrome control rather than a destination.WCAG 2.4.3 Placing it visually at the top while leaving it last in the markup is the fix that looks free and is not: focus would jump backwards up the rail. Real, but small — one Tab.
Discovery
→ neither
The case against the bottom is that it falls below the fold on a long nav. This nav is seven items. It does not reach the fold at any viewport we support, so the argument does not apply — it would if this were the admin tool's left-nav, which is exactly why this page does not speak for that one.
Pointer distance
→ top, weakly
The top of the rail is a place the cursor already goes — the logo and the profile are there. The bottom is a dedicated trip. This is a genuine point for the top, and it is worth very little, because collapsing is a once-a-week action, not a once-a-minute one. Optimising travel to a setting is how settings end up in the way of the work.
The mobile sheet
→ bottom, decisively
Under SHEET_BELOW_PX this rail stops collapsing and becomes a sheet, which needs a menu button — and that button's conventional home is the top left. Put the collapse control there too and the same pixel is two different controls at two widths: one toggles a persistent width, the other opens and closes an overlay. Nobody reads the breakpoint; they just learn that the corner is unpredictable. The bottom keeps them apart by construction.

One thing the top does better, and it is worth taking either way. Collapsed, the profile block reduces to a lone avatar with dead space beside it, and a top-placed toggle fills that row tidily. If the bottom placement stands, that gap still wants solving — probably by letting the profile row shrink rather than by moving a control into it.

Not to be confused with a third option. A chevron floating on the rail's outer edge, half over the content, is the one placement to avoid: a control that straddles the boundary between two regions belongs to neither, is the first thing people fail to find, and has no honest place in the tab order at all.

04

The tooltip contract — the part that is usually wrong

A collapsed rail makes tooltips load-bearing, and the browser's native title attribute fails every line of this table. It is not a shortcut to the pattern; it is the absence of it.

It is never the name
The button is named by its own label element. The tooltip is attached with aria-describedby, so it is read after the name, as a description. If deleting the tooltip would leave a button called "button", the wiring is wrong.
Keyboard gets it instantly
Hover waits TIP_HOVER_DELAY so that crossing the rail does not strobe. Focus does not wait at all — a keyboard user has already spent a keystroke to get there; making them hold still is making them wait for nothing.
Dismissible, hoverable, persistent
Escape closes it without moving focus. The pointer can travel onto the tooltip itself without it vanishing — hence TIP_CLOSE_GRACE. And it stays while hover or focus remains; it does not time out on its own.WCAG 1.4.13
One tooltip, not seven
A single node is moved and re-labelled, so only one can ever be open and there is one id for aria-describedby to point at. Seven tooltips means seven ways to leave one on screen.
Not on touch, and not the only route
There is no hover on a touch screen, so on a narrow viewport the rail does not collapse to icons at all — it becomes a sheet with full labels. A tooltip must never carry information that exists nowhere else.
Expanded means no tooltip
When the label is visible the tooltip is suppressed entirely. A tooltip that repeats the word next to it teaches people that tooltips contain nothing, which is what they will assume when one finally matters.

Whose choice is it, and for how long

The state persists per browser here. Per user is the honest version, and it raises the question this exploration cannot answer alone: does a learner who collapsed the rail on a laptop want it collapsed on the tablet they study on at night, where the screen is narrower and the labels matter more?

Auto-collapse, and whether it may ever override

Collapsing below a width threshold is standard and mostly right. The unsettled half is what happens when the window grows back: restoring the rail overrules a person who may have collapsed it deliberately, and not restoring it leaves them narrow forever. A remembered explicit choice has to outrank the breakpoint, which means storing why it is collapsed, not just that it is.

Seven glyphs that do not exist yet

The stage uses typographic stand-ins. Collapsed, the icon is the nav item, and "Learning Path" versus "My Courses" versus "Course Catalog" are three things one icon set has to keep visibly apart at 17px. That is an icon commission, and it is the real dependency under concept A — not the CSS.

What the badge dot is allowed to mean

Collapsed, "Behind" becomes a dot, and a dot can only say something. If a second state ever needs the same corner — an unread count, a due date — they collide, and the answer is probably that only one state in the whole rail is ever permitted a dot. Not decided.

Scope. The learner desktop rail only. The admin tool's left nav is a different problem — deeper, and it has a second level — and nothing here should be ported to it without re-asking question 03.
