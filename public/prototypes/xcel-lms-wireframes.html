01

What the brief asks for

A new platform for Insurance, Mortgage and Banking across three surfaces. This pass covers the Learner experience and the Compass hand-off — the seam where the learner leaves a surface we build, enters one Compass builds, and comes back, while believing it is one product.

Two users drive the design. The learner is a newly recruited agent doing pre-licensing before they are contracted: personal email only, no employee ID, no producer number, on a licensing deadline, on a phone, at night. The admin is an agency manager who sees only their own subtree and wants one answer — who is stuck.

The instruction that shapes this document: where a decision is open, show two options rather than picking one, and flag open product questions rather than inventing answers. And if only one thing gets done, it is the Compass hand-off and inside it the interruption state.

The state the brief would start with A learner is 40 minutes into a 90-minute exam simulation, three days from a licensing deadline, on a phone, and the save is slow. Section 05 draws that state. A version of exactly this ejected learners mid-exam in August, which is why the platform is being rebuilt.

Moved mid-course

A learner migrated from the old platform.
Not designed
Blocked on an open product question. Whether a learner's course progress carries over when they are moved to the new platform mid-course is undecided — and it changes this arrival entirely. Carried-over progress means a resume screen; no carry-over means a restart, which needs an explanation and probably an apology.
Depends on: "Whether a learner's course progress carries over if they are moved mid-course" — brief, open questions.

05

The interruption state

The brief's "if you only get to one thing"

40 minutes into a 90-minute exam simulation, three days from a licensing deadline, on a phone, and the save is slow. Frames at 390px. The single governing rule is below, and every frame is an application of it.

The rule A slow save must never look like a lost session. Which means, concretely: no full-screen spinner, no modal, no dimming, nothing that hides the question or stops the timer. The learner keeps answering while we sort ourselves out, and we say so in a strip that does not move.

6

Compass unavailable

Not designed
The recovery depends on what Compass can promise. If the frame holds answers locally, this is a wait. If it does not, this is data loss — and the honest screen for data loss mid-exam is a different screen, with a different apology and a route to a re-sit.
Depends on: the containment contract, and specifically whether answers survive the frame becoming unreachable.
This is the August failure. Drawing an optimistic version of it would be the same mistake the rebuild exists to correct.
06

Licensure hand-off — the exam as a task type

Learner flow 06 · specced in full separately

The most important moment in the journey happens outside the product. It is also the moment the study plan exists to serve — so it belongs in the calendar the plan already lives in, as a task the learner fills in before and after. The existing Study Calendar already treats the exam date as the anchor everything derives from; what follows adds the booking, the sit, the result and the retake.

The one structural change Every task on this calendar today is binary — done or not done. An exam is not: fail is not incomplete. It is resolved, with an outcome, and it spawns a retake. So the verb is "Record result", never "Mark as complete". Everything else in this section flows through branches that already exist.

10

Multi-state licensure

Not designed
One learner, several states, several exams. This section models one attempt series against one calendar. The brief's Records flow implies more — hours held across states with different requirements and renewal dates.
Needs a decision on whether a learner has one calendar per licence or one calendar with several anchors — which is a data-model question, not a layout one.
One naming trap, found in the existing code kind: 'exam' is already taken — it means the practice exam, and every instance is course-linked with a link into the LMS. The external licensing exam is its opposite in every respect, so it needs its own key ('licensing-exam'). Overloading the existing one would still typecheck, and the calendar would silently treat a real licensing exam as a practice one.

08

How to read this

Fidelity is deliberate. Greyscale, no brand colour, real proportions. The rails, bars and cards sit at the measurements the existing platform uses, so a frame here maps onto something buildable — but nothing is styled, because the brief asked for structure and the fastest way to lose a structural review is to give it a colour to argue about.

The two reading modes reorder this page; they do not hide anything. Every section stays in the document in both modes, so find-in-page, select-all, and print all still reach the whole thing.

Hatched frames are the point, not gaps in the work. Each one names what it is blocked on and who owns the answer. Drawing an optimistic version of any of them would resolve an open question by implication — which is what the brief asked us not to do.

Green notes mark something that already exists. Violet notes are design reasoning. Amber is unresolved.
