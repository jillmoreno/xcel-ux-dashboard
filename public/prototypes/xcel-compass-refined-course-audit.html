Compass Refined — Your Course

A UX audit of the Refined version’s course page, 2026–11–12 · read against the actual Florida Life & Health section list

The page was audited straight after the real course list replaced the demo one. That order matters: most of what follows was invisible while the page carried its own nineteen hand-typed chapters, and became obvious the moment it had to describe a course that exists.

The finding behind most of the others. Every problem below is one shape: a string sitting beside the data instead of reading it. A status word, a ring’s arc, a resume card, a chapter count — each was typed once against a course that has since changed, and none of them had any way to notice.

1 · What the real course changed

The page carried 19 chapters. The course has 21 chapters and 20 exams — 41 rows. Later the same day it became 44: see section 4.

The double-numbered titles from thirteen on“Life & Health Chapter 13 | Health Chapter 4” — the page had only the short names

And one thing the page had that the upload does not: a final Certification Exam. The uploaded list ends at “Chapter Exam: Florida Life and Health Laws and Rules” with no separate certification step. It has not been rebuilt on a guess — if the real course has one, it is a row in the course list and the page picks it up unchanged.

Three contradictions it exposed

  1. No chapter was in progress, yet the page offered “Resume lesson”. The chapter state came from the ported fixture, whose current chapter — “Insurers and Insurance Markets” — is not a chapter of this course.
  2. The resume card named that chapter, and a lesson inside it.
  3. The header read “Completed” beside “1 of 41 sections”, over a hard-coded 61% ring.

All three are fixed, and the page now derives state, figures and the resume point from the same object the Overview counts against.

One that could have shipped silently. The ported outline and the real course agree on almost every chapter and diverge on one: its Chapter 2 is “Insurers and Insurance Markets”, the course’s is “Nature of Insurance”. Mapping lessons by position put lessons about stock and mutual insurers under a chapter about the nature of insurance — the right heading over the wrong contents, with nothing on screen to correct it. Lessons are matched by title now, and a chapter whose contents are not known shows none.

2 · What is working

The three-level structure survives 41 rows

Chapter → lessons → exam, with collapse, is the right shape for a course this long. No flat list would be navigable at this length, and the expanded chapter is the only place lesson-level detail belongs.

The locked exam row explains itself

“Opens after all 7 lessons” is the page’s own promise kept — the lead says a locked step tells you exactly what opens it, and the exam rows do.

The eyebrow / title split absorbed the double-numbering for free

“Life & Health Chapter 13 | Health Chapter 4” sits in the eyebrow where a chapter number belongs; the title stays “Health and Accident Insurance”. A single-line title would have been unreadable.

4 · How the course ends — built 2026–11–12

Three sections the course needed and the list did not carry: a Final Exam, a Survey, and a Certificate — the tail of the regulated sequence the fixtures already name (lessons, affidavit, final exam, survey, certificate). They are rows 42–44 of compassRefinedCourse.ts now, and the page’s ending is derived from whatever the chapters do not consume rather than typed: a closing row lists itself, and one inserted mid-course does not fall off the end.

What they replaced was a hand-written Certification Exam card carried over from Jeff’s build — an exam the course list does not contain, noted “Opens after all 19 chapters” against twenty-one of them.

⚠ The three titles are ours and want confirming. “Final Exam” is what the stage fixtures call it; “Course Survey” and “Certificate” are plain descriptions. If the live course names them differently, they are three strings in one file.

5 · One thing the audit did not catch

Chapter 1’s exam read “Completed · Review exam” inside a chapter marked In progress whose seven lessons were all still to do. Its done came from Jeff’s fixture, where that chapter was finished. One learner cannot have passed the exam for a chapter they are halfway through — the exam’s state is the course’s progress now, like everything else on the page.
