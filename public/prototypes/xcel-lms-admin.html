01

What the brief asks for

Six admin flows, and the brief is blunt about them: this is where we are furthest behind competitors, and if only one screen is designed well it should be who is stuck — a screen it says does not exist anywhere today.

The admin is an agency manager in a distribution hierarchy — agent, general agent, managing general agent, regional and above — with recruits flowing in continuously and moving between agencies. They manage by cohort and by roll-up, not learner by learner, they often pay for the seats their recruits use, and they will only ever see their own part of the tree.

FlowThe design problem the brief names
01 · The organisationTrees are deep, wide and constantly changing, and an admin sees only their subtree. A conventional tree control will not survive contact with a real hierarchy. Every roll-up is answerable "as of today" or "as it was".
02 · Setting up an agencyMake inheritance visible. Pricing set high in the tree flows down unless overridden — an admin must see at a glance whether a price is this agency's own or inherited, and what changing it affects beneath.
03 · Inviting learnersBulk is the real case. Design the whole arc — composing, previewing, sending, then seeing what happened. The follow-up view matters more than the send.
04 · Seats and entitlementsSponsored access is invisible to the learner and central to the admin. Show spend and utilisation without making the learner's experience feel metered.
05 · Cohort reportingA line in the data where a customer moved platforms, and figures either side coming from different systems. Show provenance honestly without making every screen look hedged.
06 · Who is stuckBought and never started, stalled mid-course, or approaching a deadline they will miss — answering "who, how bad, and what do I do about it" in one view, at cohort scale.
02

One table, not three

The claim this document is built on Flows 03, 04 and 06 are not three screens. They are one roster asked three different questions — and PartnerHub's All Learners table already answers all three, because who paid for a seat is a column, not a mode. A learner whose agency bought their seat and a learner who bought their own are the same row with a different value in one cell. The admin's question is identical either way: how is this person doing.

What that buys is unusually concrete. PartnerHub's five status badges — Compliant · On Track · At Risk · Overdue · Not Started — are already, word for word, the vocabulary flow 06 needs. They were not designed for insurance and they fit anyway, which is the tell that the underlying job is the same: someone managing other people's compliance, scoped to a subtree whose boundaries they do not control.

The additions are narrow: a Paid by column, a Seat column that only means anything when the agency paid, and a deadline-derived risk the badges do not yet compute. Everything else — the badges, the progress cell, the pill tabs, the side panel, the kebab, bulk selection — exists.

Spend is the one thing that is not a column, because it is a total rather than a property of a learner. It belongs on the agency roll-up above the table — which keeps it out of the list, and off the learner's screen, by construction.

04

The organisation

Flow 01 · drill, don't unfold

The brief says a conventional tree control will not survive a real hierarchy. It is right — and a table-first product already knows the alternative. Each level is a filtered list with a breadcrumb, not an expanding node. You drill; you do not unfold.

Why not a tree

A tree control asks the admin to hold the shape in their head and keeps every branch one click from open. At six levels and a few hundred agencies that is unusable, and it makes subtree scoping a rendering rule rather than a fact. A breadcrumb makes scoping structural: you can only ever be somewhere below your own root, and the path you took is on screen.

"As of" is the same problem as provenance (flow 05), and it should have one mechanism, not two. Both are asking what does this number actually mean. Two separate hedging devices would make every screen look uncertain, which is exactly what the brief warns against — so the basis is stated once, where the number is read.
There is a working precedent for that in the certificate reporting status variants — reported / pending roster / non-reporting. A per-row disclosure of what we know about a record, rather than a caveat draped over the page.

06

Drawn as holes

Flows 02 · 05

Two of the six have no analog in either project, and both are about the agency rather than the learner — so they sit above the roster rather than changing it. Neither is drawn, for the reasons each names.

A

Inherited vs overridden pricing

Not designed
The blast radius is the hard part, not the badge. Showing that a price is inherited is a label. Showing what changing it will affect beneath is a preview of an edit across an unknown number of agencies and in-flight invites — and whether an override applies to seats already sold is a commercial question, not a design one.
Needs: does an override apply to existing enrolments, or only to new ones?
The one place it cannot be deferred is the invite composer (§05 frame 1), which already shows the price and its origin — because the admin is about to send something carrying a price they may not have set.
B

Provenance across the migration line

Not designed
The mechanism is settled; the threshold is not. Provenance and "as of" should share one basis line (§04). What is undecided is when a figure is uncertain enough to say so — mark everything and every screen looks hedged, which the brief explicitly rejects; mark nothing and the first wrong number costs the trust.
Needs: the confidence rule. Which figures carry lower confidence, and by what test?
C

Utilisation, beyond claimed

Not designed
Claimed is not used. Gift Recipients models two seat states — claimed and unclaimed — and its own open question is what "claimed" can actually report from the LMS. A rep can log in once and never return: that seat is claimed, paid for, and idle. The roster above shows a third state, Idle, but the rule behind it is invented.
Needs: what counts as activity, and after how long does a claimed seat read as idle?
This is where flows 04 and 06 meet. "Am I wasting money" and "who is stuck" are the same row read by two questions — which is the strongest argument in the document for not building them as separate screens.
