01

Learner — what the brief asks for

Learner

A new platform for Insurance, Mortgage and Banking across three surfaces. This pass covers the Learner experience and the Compass hand-off — the seam where the learner leaves a surface we build, enters one Compass builds, and comes back, while believing it is one product.

Two users drive the design. The learner is a newly recruited agent doing pre-licensing before they are contracted: personal email only, no employee ID, no producer number, on a licensing deadline, on a phone, at night. The admin is an agency manager who sees only their own subtree and wants one answer — who is stuck.

The instruction that shapes this document: where a decision is open, show two options rather than picking one, and flag open product questions rather than inventing answers. And if only one thing gets done, it is the Compass hand-off and inside it the interruption state.

The state the brief would start with A learner is 40 minutes into a 90-minute exam simulation, three days from a licensing deadline, on a phone, and the save is slow. Section 05 draws that state. A version of exactly this ejected learners mid-exam in August, which is why the platform is being rebuilt.

03

Admin — what the brief asks for

Admin

Six admin flows, and the brief is blunt about them: this is where we are furthest behind competitors, and if only one screen is designed well it should be who is stuck — a screen it says does not exist anywhere today.

The admin is an agency manager in a distribution hierarchy — agent, general agent, managing general agent, regional and above — with recruits flowing in continuously and moving between agencies. They manage by cohort and by roll-up, not learner by learner, they often pay for the seats their recruits use, and they will only ever see their own part of the tree.

FlowThe design problem the brief names
01 · The organisationTrees are deep, wide and constantly changing, and an admin sees only their subtree. A conventional tree control will not survive contact with a real hierarchy. Every roll-up is answerable "as of today" or "as it was".
02 · Setting up an agencyMake inheritance visible. Pricing set high in the tree flows down unless overridden — an admin must see at a glance whether a price is this agency's own or inherited, and what changing it affects beneath.
03 · Inviting learnersBulk is the real case. Design the whole arc — composing, previewing, sending, then seeing what happened. The follow-up view matters more than the send.
04 · Seats and entitlementsSponsored access is invisible to the learner and central to the admin. Show spend and utilisation without making the learner's experience feel metered.
05 · Cohort reportingA line in the data where a customer moved platforms, and figures either side coming from different systems. Show provenance honestly without making every screen look hedged.
06 · Who is stuckBought and never started, stalled mid-course, or approaching a deadline they will miss — answering "who, how bad, and what do I do about it" in one view, at cohort scale.

05

How to read this

This document is an extract, not a third opinion. Sections 01–04 are lifted verbatim from the two wireframe companions so that the ask and the inventory can be read in one pass, without the drawn screens in between. Where the two disagree, the companions win — they are where the reasoning lives.

Coverage means one thing throughout: Built is a surface you can open today and point at; Partial is the right furniture answering a different question, so the work is the difference rather than the screen; None has no analog anywhere in either project. The admin inventory uses two additional labels of its own — Most of it and The roster half — which sit at the Built and Partial levels respectively, and the filter treats them that way.

The counts in the masthead are derived from the tables below on load, not authored, so editing a row moves them.

What this extract deliberately drops: every wireframe, the hatched holes, and both open registers. For the learner arc see the learner wireframes; for the one-roster argument and the six flows drawn, the admin wireframes.
