Study Pace & Readiness — two widgets, two different questions

The dashboard's second row has two empty cards. They look alike and they are not: Study Pace asks will you get through the coursework before your exam date, and every word on it derives from lessons finished per week against lessons left and days left. Readiness asks of what you have covered, what has not held up, and it derives from chapter quizzes and, later, the practice exam. One is about time; the other is about knowledge. Neither shows a percentage.

Four demo axes above. Progress and Their pace drive Study Pace; Quizzes and Practice exam drive Readiness. Every date, minute and topic on this page is recomputed from them — nothing is authored — so the widgets can be judged in every state they can reach rather than the one that photographs well. Section 02 adds the moment before any of that: the recommended pace, set from the 30-day access window on day one.

02

Before there is a pace — the recommendation

Added 2026-09-21. On day one Study Pace has nothing to measure, and the two references for this moment both ask the learner something first — a study style (Thorough / Average / Quick) or an hours-per-week preset. This widget tells instead. The course comes with 30 days of access: that is a date the learner already bought, not a number they have to judge, so the recommendation is derived from it and arrives set. The only decision left is whether to adjust.

Demo · day of access
There is no Relaxed option, and that is a finding, not an omission. The reference's Aggressive / Recommended / Relaxed presets assume the course waits for you. Inside a 30-day window the slowest pace that still finishes is the recommendation — anything gentler means buying an extension, which is a purchase, not a pace. The Adjust panel therefore only lets you finish earlier, spread the work across more or fewer nights, or change how you study; it never offers a slower plan.
Taken further on its own page. Pace presets takes the other reading of the window — aim at all 30 days, so Relaxed / Recommended / Focused become three dates — and shows four UI variants of it across 12-, 20- and 42-lesson courses.
Study style is an adjustment, not a gate. Thorough / Average / Quick is worth keeping from the reference because it changes the honest minutes-per-lesson figure — notes and normal-speed video genuinely cost more. But asking it up front, before the learner has opened a lesson, is the same day-zero question the study-plan page argues against. Here it sits under Adjust with Average pre-selected.
03

Study Pace — every state it can be in

Each row is the same renderer fed a fixed situation, so the copy is derived, not typed. The verdict comes from one ratio: lessons a week you actually do, over lessons a week the exam date now requires. Minutes are only the translation of that ratio into an evening — never the thing being measured. Time in the product is not on this widget at all; an open tab is not studying.

The tolerance band is deliberate. Between PACE_TOLERANCE_INVENTED and 1.0 the learner is slightly behind and the widget says On track anyway — the coursework buffer before the exam is what makes that honest. A widget that changes colour for one slow week teaches the learner to stop reading it, and then has nothing left to say when it matters. Same rule as the study plan's drift states.
Behind recommends more time and nothing else. That was the call for this widget: one suggestion, no fork. It quietly assumes the exam date is fixed — reasonable for a booked state exam, and the study-plan page argues the opposite for a self-set target. The one state where the widget cannot honour that is Not enough time: when even the ceiling pace does not land before the exam, the only honest sentence mentions the date.
04

Readiness — two ways to name what needs another look

Same data, same state, three treatments. A names the chapter the learner studied, so every line has a Resume target. B names the domain the state exam is weighted by, so the list is about the exam rather than the course. The difference is not cosmetic: a chapter can feed two domains and a domain can span two chapters, and only one of these lists can be clicked straight back into a lesson.

A

Chapter titles

For: each line resumes a lesson. Against: the exam does not test chapters; a learner who reviews "Life Policy Types" may still be soft on the domain it half-covers.

B

State exam outline domains

For: speaks the exam's language, and the practice exam already reports this way. Against: a domain flagged by one shaky chapter stays flagged until every chapter in it holds — and the Review link has to pick one chapter to land on.

C

A score, with the pass mark

For: one number is legible at a glance, and "70 to pass" gives it a meaning the learner already understands from every exam they have sat. Against: it says how much but not what — the topic list is the actionable half and this has none — and the reference it comes from blends completion into the score, which makes a progress number wear a readiness badge. Here the score is quiz and practice-exam results only; progress is Study Pace's job.

05

Readiness — the ladder, and when the word "ready" is allowed

Before the practice exam there is no verdict: the widget lists topics or says there is nothing to list, and never says ready. The practice exam is what earns the word, because it is the only instrument that covers the whole outline — including lessons the learner has not reached.

06

What each widget is not

Study Pace

Time · derived from lessons and dates
  • Not time on task. Minutes spent in lessons is noisy and unflattering; lessons completed is what "on track" actually means. Minutes appear only as the translation of a pace.
  • Not a plan editor. It reports against the plan; changing the date or the evenings lives on the Study Plan page it links to.
  • Not a per-lesson estimate. The "18 minutes" on Jump Back In is the lesson's length; this widget's minutes are what the week needs.

Readiness

Knowledge · derived from quizzes and the practice exam
  • Not Compass readiness. Compass computes a probabilistic low / medium / high we do not own. This is deterministic and ours: what you have shown us, not what we predict. If both ship, they need different names or one of them goes.
  • Not a score. The quiz percentages exist and set the threshold; they are never displayed. A learner sees topics, not numbers.
  • Not a gate. Nothing here blocks booking. It informs the decision the learner already has the right to make.

Invented rules, and the holes

The fixed-date assumption

Behind recommends more time and never questions the date. For a PSI-booked state exam that is right. For a learner whose "exam date" is still a self-set target, the study-plan page says the honest move is to price both. Which date this widget is reading needs deciding before the copy is final.

Two readiness opinions

If Compass ships its readiness signal to this dashboard, the second row has two cards answering "am I ready" with different methods. The admin roster's Risk column has the same collision. Nobody has settled which wins.

What counts as shaky

QUIZ_SHAKY_INVENTED is a threshold on a score we refuse to show. Which attempt counts — first, best, latest? A retaken quiz passed on the third try is a different fact from one passed cold, and the widget currently cannot tell them apart.

Chapters that span domains

Option B flags a domain if any covered chapter in it is shaky. New York Regulation is fed by chapters 1 and 12; a strong chapter 12 does not clear a weak chapter 1. That is probably right, but the Review link then has to choose a chapter to land on.

What the score is made of

Option C averages fixture quiz scores and blends the practice exam. The reference it answers folds course completion in too, and that is the trap: a learner at 40% complete with perfect quizzes would score badly for a reason that has nothing to do with knowledge. If a score ships, its ingredients have to be stated on the card, and completion should not be one of them.

Minutes per lesson vs credit hours

The recommendation prices a lesson at MINS_PER_LESSON_INVENTED (35 min). The storefront publishes 40 credit hours for the same 42 lessons — about 57 min each. If seat-time is the real figure, every nightly ask on this page is ~1.6× too light, and the day-one recommendation becomes nearly three hours a night. Which number the product believes has to be settled before this copy is trusted.

What "Extend access" is

The Won’t-fit state routes to an extension because nothing else is honest. Whether one exists as a product, what it costs, and whether a learner on an agency-sponsored seat can buy it themselves are all undecided — and the last one is the same "who pays" hole the catalogue has.

The trailing window

Observed pace is lessons in the last PACE_WINDOW_DAYS_INVENTED days. A learner who front-loaded and then paused reads as Paused even if they are miles ahead. Whether "ahead overall, stalled lately" deserves its own sentence is open.

Nothing on this page is an authored figure. Dates derive from days-left and lessons-left; minutes derive from lessons-per-week and MINS_PER_LESSON_INVENTED; topic lists derive from the quiz and practice-exam fixtures. Guarded by smoke/smoke-pace-readiness.mjs, which asserts the relationships — behind is only said when the projection lands after the date, "ready" never appears before the practice exam, no percentage sign appears inside either widget — rather than string matches.
