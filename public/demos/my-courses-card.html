02 · The reasoning

Fourteen changes, and the failure each one prevents

Every row is a change that shipped, the state it replaced, and why — the usability problem it answers, not the day it was decided. The tag under each reason names the principle it rests on.

Was
Now
Why
Four live targets on one card: a full-width button, the title as a link, the card ground, and a ⋮ menu in the file.
One target. The card. Every link and button that remains stops its own click.
A learner deciding which course to open should make one decision, not pick between four doors to the same room. Four targets also means four hit areas to learn, four hover states to read, and a mis-click that lands somewhere the learner didn't intend. Hick's law · recognition over recall
A finished course drew a disabled "Completed" button — a dead control sitting where every other card has a live one.
A Certificate link at the foot of a completed card — the award glyph, the course's certificate, or the certificates page when none is issued.
A disabled button is the page telling a learner they have arrived somewhere there is nothing to do, which is false: a finished course has a certificate. It was the one dead end on the page, and the PRD's "No dead ends" rules it out. No dead ends · user control and freedom
State, category and line of authority moved off the card into the sheet on 18 November — the card said only its title and percentage.
All three back on the card as one ruled row, the state abbreviated: FL | Licensing | Life and Health.
A learner with thirteen enrolments scans for which course, and the three facts that distinguish two Florida courses from each other were a click away on every card. Values only, abbreviated state, hairlines between — the shared card's meta row, so the line rarely wraps. Stacked under icons and one labelled line per fact were both drawn and both rejected: each spent three rows on what one says. Scannability · progressive disclosure, correctly placed
An expired or failed course read "Progress Made" beside a pill reading "Expired - 04/20/2026".
"Expired" and "Failed" — the words the pill already carries.
Two labels for one state that disagreed with each other. The case for "Progress Made" was that the bar shows how far the learner got; the marker on the unfilled track makes that point now, and the percentage beside the status still does. One name per thing. Match between system and the real world · consistency
A pill on every card, at full catalog strength — including "In Progress" and "Not Started", which the status row already says.
A pill only where there is something to say beyond the status row: Completed, Failed, Expired, and a live course inside its last seven usable days. Tints mixed half with the card's ground.
A grid where every card wears a badge is a grid where no badge means anything. Reserving the pill for the four states that need an extra word makes the amber countdown read as urgent again. The record's own Due Soon badge fires at the catalogue's sixty days — too early for a pill that says act this week. Signal economy · alerts that don't cry wolf
The expired pill in pale pink — Error 100, the same fill as Failed.
Light grey, primary ink, carrying its date: "Expired - 04/20/2026".
A pale pink pill read as a warning still in play. Grey reads as over, and the date on it says when — so a learner can tell at a glance whether a course needs attention or has simply ended. Colour encodes state, not alarm
An expired or failed bar drew a Primary 200 fill — a filled bar on a course that cannot progress.
No fill. A 2px charcoal marker on the empty grey track, at the point the learner reached.
A filled bar says "this is how far along you are"; a marker says "this is how far you got". On a course whose access has ended, the second is the true sentence. Visibility of system status
The track was a 1px Primary 400 hairline through the middle of the bar's box.
A 5px Neutral 200 track with the fill over it, 2.5 corners.
A hairline gives the eye nothing to measure against, so 30% and 45% looked alike across a row. A full-weight track makes the remainder visible, which is the half of a progress bar that carries the information. Perceptual accuracy
Titles were Bold 22 / 24 and ran to whatever length they needed, so each card's rows sat at a different height.
Bold 18 / 20, clamped to three lines with an ellipsis, inside a fixed 60px box — a short title centred in it.
An unbounded title pushes everything under it out of alignment, and across a row of four the labels, dates and bars stopped lining up. One box per title keeps the rules level; centring a short one holds it on the image's centre line instead of hugging the photo. The full title stays the card's accessible name. Alignment as comparison aid
No date on the card at all. A learner could not tell when access ends without opening the course.
One access line while the course is live: Access Ends: 05/17/2026, (5 days) — label at 400, date and count at 700.
The deadline is the thing that makes a learner act, and it was the one fact the card did not carry. The count is usable days — expiry minus one, the figure the study plan is built on — so the card and the countdown pill can never disagree. Said in mm/dd/yyyy, matching the Home course head the card opens onto. Visibility of system status · one deadline, said one way
A finished card carried its date block too — a completion date, a score line, an expiry line, all at once.
The date block comes off an expired card. Its date is on the pill, its progress is the bar's marker.
Each fact once. The score is on the pill; the completion date is on the status row; the end of access is on the Expired pill. A card that says the same thing twice makes a reader check whether the two agree. Say each thing once
The finished card stepped back to a different ground — Secondary 100 against the live cards' Primary 100.
One fill for every status — Secondary 100 lightened half with Neutral 50, a 1px Primary 100 rule inside the edge.
Status said by the card's colour is status said in a channel a colour-blind reader cannot read and a greyscale print loses. The pill and the status row say it in words instead, and one material lets the grid read as one library. Never colour alone · WCAG 1.4.1
The card was clickable with nothing but a cursor to show it.
A hover lift (−1px), a Primary 500 ring at 40%, a soft shadow, every line of text to the primary ink, and a 2px focus ring on keyboard focus.
An affordance a learner discovers by accident is an affordance most of them never discover. Enter and Space on the focused card do what a click does; prefers-reduced-motion drops the lift and keeps the ring. Affordance · keyboard parity
The two text links were Bold 700 at 11/16 with no padding — the loudest text on the card, in a 18px-tall hit area.
600 on the body's 18px line, with 3px of padding above and below pulled back out by margin: a 24px hit area that moves nothing.
Two links shouting over a serif title inverted the card's hierarchy, and an 18px target fails WCAG 2.2's minimum. The padding buys the height without costing a pixel of layout. WCAG 2.2 target size · typographic hierarchy
The card was a four-row CSS subgrid, so every card's title row took the tallest title in the grid row.
A flex column, 8px between blocks, the progress pinned low with margin-top:auto.
On the subgrid, the space under one card's content was 8px and its neighbour's 52px, and no padding could make it even. Off it, each card owns its spacing. The cost, stated plainly: bars and date lines no longer sit at one height across a row — accepted, because nothing at the foot needs to line up now that the buttons are gone. The trade admitted, not hidden
A description was drawn under the title for a day, with "Description coming soon" as its fallback — section 3 of the PRD asks for one on every card.
No description. The records keep their text; the sheet is where a longer read belongs.
Three lines of prose on a card that already carries a title, three labels, a date, a status and a bar buries the facts a learner scans for. This is a deviation from the PRD and it is logged as one — not an omission. Scannability over completeness
⚠
One change is a known cost, not an improvement. Hiding the card's own buttons means Begin, Resume, Retake and Purchase Extension are now two clicks away — the card opens Home, or "Details" opens the sheet that carries them. That was the trade for a single click target, and the sheet exists to keep those actions reachable without leaving the page.

03 · Interaction

Where a learner can click

Three live targets at most, and only on a completed card. Everything else on the card is text. The rule that holds it together: every link and button stops its own click, so only a click on the card's ground opens Home — and Enter or Space on the focused card does the same thing.

Certificate Pending — not a link

When an affidavit, survey or proctor's sign-off is still owed, the slot reads Certificate Pending with an hourglass, in the status row's own text. It is deliberately not clickable: there is nothing to open yet, and the sheet offers no View Certificate either.

Details → — on every card

Bottom right. Opens the card's own Course Details sheet from the right at 480px, which carries the course's buttons, the deadline box, the description and the outline. The sheet renders outside the card's element, so a click inside it cannot bubble back through the portal and open Home.

Keyboard

The card takes focus (tabIndex 0) and shows a 2px Primary 500 ring at 2px offset. Enter and Space open Home — guarded by e.target === e.currentTarget, so a key press inside the Certificate link or the Details button does not also fire the card.

onKeyDown · preventDefault() on Enter / Space

—

Built and switched off — do not draw these

A developer will find them in the file. The ⋮ menu (Go to Course Home, Download Certificate) behind SHOW_CARD_MENU = false; the full-width button per status, View Certificate in the button's clothes, and Review Course › under the title, all behind SHOW_CARD_ACTIONS = false. Each is kept for the record with an archive row, not revived.

How the status is resolved

One pass, in this order, and at most one answer: Expired → Due Soon → Failed → Completed → In Progress → Not Started. Then one demotion: a Due Soon card whose usable days exceed seven is drawn as In Progress, because the record's own badge fires at the catalogue's sixty days and that is too early for a pill that says act this week.

Nothing is invented

A completed record with no score wears a plain Completed pill; one with no completion date reads Completed alone on the status row. A failed record with no score wears Failed; an expired one with no date, Expired. A record with no access date draws no access line at all — and none in XCEL has been without one since 23 November.

What the sheet inherited

Four things moved into it as the card got quieter, and each one is why the sheet exists rather than a second page:

The course's buttons — the same set Home's course head offers when this course is active, so a learner who wants to resume without leaving My Courses still can.

The deadline and the extension, in their own tiered box above the buttons, rather than a button in the row that says nothing about urgency.

The description, under its own sub-header — the long read the card gave up.

The outline — nice to have, not needed for MVP. It is the one slot that can ship later without the sheet losing its job. It should not be a hard lift when it does: it is the same six rows, the same data, the same journey helpers Home already draws on the home screen — so the sheet and Home tick the same rows for the same percentage rather than two implementations agreeing by luck.

Three facts once stood here as labelled rows of their own. They were removed on 24 November: the ruled row under the title says them once.

The sheet before this one

The 18 November card opened AtlasCourseDetailsSheet — the same head, and a body holding only the three facts as a labelled list plus the package paragraph. No progress, no deadline box, no buttons, no outline. It is still in the code as the other arm of the flag, and still what "Course Details ›" under that card's title opens.

06 · Before you build

Handle with care

⚠
Dead code lives in the file on purpose. SHOW_CARD_MENU, SHOW_CARD_ACTIONS and BUTTON_EXPIRED are all false or unused, and the blocks behind them are complete. They are kept for the record with archive rows. Don't draw them, and don't treat finding them as a signal they are coming back.
⚠
The original card is never edited. The PRD card is a copy — AtlasCourseCardFigmaV2.tsx — and the flag picks between the two at the call site. Changing the 18 November card to "keep them in sync" breaks the only side-by-side comparison the page has.
⚠
The day count is usable days, not days until. Expiry minus one, the figure the study plan is built on. The access line, the countdown pill and the deadline box all read the same helper, which is the only reason they cannot disagree. A fresh Math.round on two dates reintroduces an off-by-one that only shows up on the last day.
⚠
Archived courses are not on this page. Archiving is a place, not a status, and an archived card draws identically to an active one — so with the View Archived link hidden, an archived course here would be indistinguishable from a live one. Whether that is right is still open.
