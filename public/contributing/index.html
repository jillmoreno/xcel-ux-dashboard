Part 0What this is

This page gets you from nothing to your first change in front of the team. Read it once, on your first day. After that, the day-to-day guide is the one you keep open.

There are only three things to understand, and the rest of this page is detail about them.

The Hub

A website where everyone — designers, developers, stakeholders — sees the team's work. It has a list of the product as it stands, and a board where work under discussion is reviewed.

Your branch

Your own private copy of the project. You change it as much as you like. Nothing on it is seen by anyone until you choose to share it, and nothing on it changes the product until Jill decides it does.

Claude

The desktop app you type sentences to. It does every technical step for you — copying the project, saving your work, publishing it — so you never type a command.

You design. Claude does the technical parts. Jill decides what ships.You never type a command, you never run the website on your own computer, and you never touch the copy of the project that everyone sees.

Part 1The whole thing in one picture

Your work travels left to right. Every box is a place; every arrow is one sentence you say to Claude, or one click.

In words, left to right:

  1. You change the project in a folder on your computer, by talking to Claude.
  2. Claude sends your branch to GitHub when you say "push". GitHub is the online copy; your branch there is yours alone.
  3. Netlify builds your branch into a website at its own address, a few minutes after every push. That address is your preview. You never run the site yourself.
  4. You add that address to Refinement on the Hub. The moment you do, everyone with the Hub's link can open it.
  5. The team looks and talks. You change, push again; the address stays the same.
  6. Jill ships it. When a piece is decided, she puts your branch into the product. Prototypes then shows it, and you start the next piece on a fresh branch.
Most Important Thing. You work on YOUR BRANCH. Never push directly to main."Push" always means your branch. Main is the copy everyone sees, and only Jill puts anything there. If you ever find yourself on main, stop and tell her.

The one-sentence version. Branch, build, share, discuss, ship. The day-to-day guide walks that loop every day; this page gets you to the start of it.

Part 3Setting up your computer

Once, on your first day. Four sentences to Claude, each with a copy button when you hover it.

  1. Connect the folder.

    Open the Claude desktop app, go to the Code tab, and choose the empty folder you made. From now on, Claude can read and change what is in it.

  2. Copy the project down.
    YOUClone the GitHub repository jillmoreno/xcel-ux-dashboard into this folder.

    "Clone" means copy. A minute later the folder is full of the project's files. You will not read them; Claude will.

  3. Install what the project needs.
    YOUInstall the project's dependencies.

    The project leans on a few hundred small pieces of code other people wrote. This fetches them. A few minutes; you only do it once, and Claude will tell you if it ever needs doing again.

  4. Put your name on your work.
    YOUFor this project, set my git name to Your Name and my git email to you@scorebuilders.com.

    Use your real name and your work email. Every change you save carries this name, so the team can see whose it is. Skip it and your work shows up as "Claude".

  5. Check.
    YOUWhich branch am I on, and is the project up to date?

    Claude should say you are on main and up to date. That is the one time you should be on main — Part 7 moves you off it before you change anything.

Nothing here runs the website. You will never start the site on your own computer, and you do not need to. Your preview is the branch build in Part 1, which Netlify makes for you.

You work on your branch. Jill works on main. The one rule under every word above — it is the stop sign in Part 1.

Part 5Where your work lives

Two kinds of work, two homes. Picking the wrong one is the most common mistake here, so say which kind it is when you ask Claude to build it.

You are makingSay to ClaudeWhat it isReviewers open
A piece of the product"a React component, behind a feature flag that is on by default on this branch"A page, a card, a panel, or a new version of something that already exists, in the app's own code.Your branch build at /dashboard-rebrand?demo=1
A standalone page to look at"a standalone HTML page in public/demos"A single hand-made web page, not wired into the app. An idea, a comparison, a brief answered in pictures.Your branch build at /demos/your-file.html

Never public/prototypes/. That folder holds finished explorations that each have a row on the Hub and their own tests. Work in review goes in public/demos/, which exists for exactly that.

Changing something that already exists? Do not edit it. Ask Claude to copy it to a sibling and build on the copy, with a switch choosing between the two. That way you and the person editing the original never collide. The routine /promote-component does this for you.

Part 6The one rule: switches

Every change to the product sits behind a switch. That one idea is what lets five designers work on one product without anyone's half-finished work appearing in front of stakeholders.

  1. On your branch, the switch is on. Your branch build shows your work.
  2. On main, the switch is off. The product looks exactly as it did.
  3. When Jill ships your branch, she decides the switch. On, and it is in the product. Off, and the code is there but nothing shows until someone decides otherwise.

You get all of this by asking for it in the same breath as the work:

YOUBuild it behind a feature flag that is on by default on this branch.

Then ask "What is the flag called?" and write it down. Reviewers can flip any switch live on your build from the robot icon on the dark bar, so you can show both versions from one address.

Why not just add it to Prototypes? Prototypes holds one row per surface, all pointing at main, and a test refuses any other row. Your work reaches Prototypes by its switch turning on in main, never by adding a row.

Part 7Your first change, start to finish

Set up and ready? This is the loop from Part 1 with the actual sentences. The day-to-day guide has the long version; this is enough for day one.

  1. Get off main and onto a branch of your own.
    YOUFetch origin, then make a new branch called feat/short-name from origin/main and switch to it.

    Name it feat/ and two or three words, lowercase, joined with dashes. Claude confirms you are on it.

  2. Say what you want, the way you would to a developer beside you.

    Name the page, the component, what it should do instead, and which kind of work it is (Part 5). Ask for the switch (Part 6). If Claude names a file you do not recognise, ask "What is that file?" before saying yes.

  3. Share it.
    YOU/share-branch

    One routine does the rest: it checks the work, saves it, pushes it, waits for Netlify to build your branch, and opens the Refinement form already filled in. You read it over and click Add link. Then it opens a card for the To Do Board; click Add card.

  4. Look at it the way a reviewer would.

    Open your row on the Hub and click through from the page a reviewer would start on, not straight to your component. Things that are invisible on a direct link are obvious on the first click.

  5. Keep going.

    Each further change is step 2 and then /share-branch again; the address stays the same. When the next change is really a different piece of work, start a fresh branch for it.

That is a chunk. Finish a chunk, share it, start a fresh branch for the next. Small branches are the habit the whole Hub is built around.

Part 8Things never to do

Short, because Claude refuses most of them for you and says why.

  • Never push to main. Every push to main rebuilds the Hub for everyone. Be on your branch (Part 7, step 1) before asking Claude to push anything. You cannot do it by accident: Claude refuses the command and says whose step it is, and GitHub refuses the push even if something slipped past Claude.
  • Never add a row to Prototypes. Your work gets there by its switch turning on in main, nothing else.
  • Never run the site on your own computer. Your branch build is the preview.
  • Never edit the Hub's own files. The project list, the demo bar, the routines. Claude refuses and names the file; ask Jill.
  • Never put review pages in public/prototypes/. Those go in public/demos/.
  • Never invent a number. A fee, a lesson count, a date XCEL has not published is marked as invented in the code and on screen. Ask instead.
  • Never share the main Hub address as your work. Send your branch build. The main address shows what is already decided, and everything else the team is doing.
